THE DAILY FIELD NOTES

Field notes update daily at midnight UTC. Each note pairs a historical citation with a verified episode covering the topic. These are independently researched facts, not quotations or transcripts from Jack’s videos. Episode topics were checked against public YouTube and Pepperbox listings. There are no invented “on this day” connections.

FOOD HISTORY / GEORGE WASHINGTON

A founding father’s breakfast order.

George Washington enjoyed hoecakes with butter and honey for breakfast. Visitors to Mount Vernon recorded the meal, which was served with hot tea.

Topic evidence: Palates of the Past — George Washington Loved His Hoe (Cakes)
Historical source: George Washington’s Mount Vernon — Hoecakes

NATURAL HISTORY / NEW ZEALAND

One name. Very different birds.

Moa were not a single species. Te Papa recognises nine, ranging from birds about the size of a large dog to giants that could reach roughly three metres tall.

Topic evidence: Jacked History — The Giant Moa Bird of New Zealand Was Huge As Fuck And Died Fast As Fuck
Historical source: Museum of New Zealand Te Papa Tongarewa — Moa

MILITARY HISTORY / GURKHAS

Yesterday’s opponents became recruits.

Gurkhas began serving Britain in 1815, during the era of the East India Company’s war with Gorkha. British officers had been impressed by the soldiers they faced in Nepal.

Topic evidence: Jacked History — Hell from the Himalayas: Gurkhas
Historical source: National Army Museum — The Gurkhas

CULTURE / HAWAIIAN COWBOYS

The cowboy champion came from Hawaiʻi.

At Cheyenne’s Frontier Days in 1908, Hawaiian paniolo Ikua Purdy won the world championship steer-roping contest with a time of 56 seconds. Archie Kaʻauʻa finished second.

Topic evidence: Jacked History — Island Cowboys: Paniolo of Hawaii
Historical source: National Park Service — The Paniolo

ANCIENT & MEDIEVAL HISTORY / SCOTLAND

A name supplied by the neighbours.

“Pict” comes from the Latin picti, meaning painted people. Historic Environment Scotland notes that there is no evidence the communities themselves used that name.

Topic evidence: Jacked History — Romans Couldn’t Contain the Picts of Scotland
Historical source: Historic Environment Scotland — Scotland’s Pictish Stones

MILITARY HISTORY / THE 82ND AIRBORNE

All American was a literal description.

The 82nd acquired its All American nickname because its original members came from all forty-eight states. The AA letters on its shoulder patch preserve that origin.

Topic evidence: Jacked History — Death From Above: The 82nd Airborne Division
Historical source: U.S. Army — 82nd Airborne Division

Two years. Two landmark laws.

The Civil Rights Act of 1964 and voting-rights legislation in 1965 were both enacted during Lyndon B. Johnson’s presidency. They appear among the landmark laws documented by the LBJ Presidential Library.

Topic evidence: Jacked History — President Lyndon Johnson Had ZERO Boundaries

EVERYDAY HISTORY / TOILETS

A royal flush, centuries ago.

In 1596, John Harington described a flushing toilet in print. He installed a working example for Elizabeth I at Richmond Palace, but the idea did not become common.

MEDIEVAL HISTORY / THE CRUSADES

A sermon helped launch a campaign.

Pope Urban II’s sermon at Clermont-Ferrand in November 1095 helped set the First Crusade in motion. The call drew participants from different social ranks, including nobles and peasants.

Topic evidence: Jacked History — The Crusades & Sultan Saladin
Historical source: The Metropolitan Museum of Art — The Crusades

A doctor before the Civil War.

MILITARY HISTORY / THE BARBARY WARS

Six ships. A new naval chapter.

In 1794, after Algerian seizures of American merchant ships, Congress authorized construction of six warships. The decision helped establish the U.S. Navy’s early fighting force.

Topic evidence: Jacked History — To the Shores of Tripoli: The Barbary Wars (America’s First Foreign Conflict)

MILITARY HISTORY / THE 1ST MARINE DIVISION

A division’s birthday aboard a battleship.

The 1st Marine Division was activated aboard USS Texas on February 1, 1941. Its organisational roots extended back to the 1st Advance Base Brigade, formed in 1913.

AMERICAN HISTORY / STAND WATIE

A signature at the centre of a rupture.

Stand Watie signed the 1835 Treaty of New Echota, which ceded Cherokee homelands in exchange for money and land in Indian Territory. The treaty faced overwhelming opposition among the Cherokee people.

CULTURE / HAWAIIAN COWBOYS

Hawaiʻi’s cowboy story has Mexican roots.

Kamehameha III invited Mexican vaqueros to teach Hawaiians cattle-handling skills. Hawaiians developed their own cowboy traditions, including distinctive saddles, gear, and music.

Topic evidence: Jacked History — Island Cowboys: Paniolo of Hawaii
